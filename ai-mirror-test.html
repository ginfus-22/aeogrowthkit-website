Why measurement comes first

Only 16% of brands track how they show up in AI search, even as McKinsey estimates $750bn in spend will flow through it by 2028. Most businesses are flying blind into the channel their buyers already use, and treating $750bn as a projection rather than a fact doesn't change the direction of travel.

Here's the thing about AI answers: you can't see them at scale by hand. You could type one question into ChatGPT and check, but your buyers ask hundreds of variations across four different models, and the answers change week to week. Guessing whether you're named is the old habit. The Mirror Test replaces it with a count.

How the Mirror Test works

01

Your real buyer questions

Not vanity keywords. The actual questions a buyer asks when they're trying to solve the problem you solve, framed the way they'd really type them.

02

Four leading models

Every question goes to ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity and Gemini. Real models, real answers, not a simulation.

03

The count

The Mirror Test records how often you're named, how often a competitor is named in your place, and which questions you're missing entirely.

04

The number

You get one clear citation rate: out of the questions you should own, how many name you today. That figure is the baseline everything else is measured against.

What you get back

Your visibility score

A single, honest number. Low to start is normal, and it's a starting line, not a verdict. What matters is that it's real and you can move it.

Who's named instead

Every time an answer names a competitor and not you, that's recorded. You see exactly who's winning the answers you want, and which questions they own.

Your gap analysis

The questions where you're absent become a ranked list of what to fix. That list is the content gap analysis Build turns into briefs.

Rank tracking measures the wrong game

A keyword rank tool tells you where a page sits on Google. Useful for the old playbook. But an AI answer doesn't show ten blue links, it gives two or three names, and the page that ranks first often isn't one of them. When Google shows an AI summary, only 8% of searches now lead to a website click, down from 15% without one.

So the question worth measuring has changed. Not "where do I rank?" but "when my buyer asks, does the AI name me?" That second question is the one the Mirror Test answers, and it's the one that decides whether AI sends you customers.
