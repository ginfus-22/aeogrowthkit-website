The problem

Most businesses have never checked whether AI mentions them. They optimised for Google for years and assumed it carried over. It doesn't. Ranking on Google and getting cited in an AI answer are different games, read off different signals, and the page that wins the search result is often the one the AI ignores.

Only 16% of brands track how they show up in AI search. So the first move isn't to fix anything. It's to find out, precisely, where you stand.

Job one

Job one: the technical audit

This isn't a score and a shrug. The audit crawls your site the way an AI answer bot does and reports, page by page, exactly what's holding you back: missing or broken schema, blocked crawlers, robots.txt rules shutting the answer bots out, structured-data gaps, indexability problems.

Then it hands you the fix. For schema, that means a clean block of code written for your page, ready to copy and paste in. No theory, no "consider implementing structured data". The actual markup, on the actual page, with the actual problem named.

Not comfortable pasting code into your site? You don't have to. A done-for-you fixing service is available, so if any of it feels like too much, you get in touch and it gets handled.

Job two

Job two: the AI Mirror Test

Here's where most tools stop short. They check your tags and call it a day. The Mirror Test checks something harder: when a real buyer asks an AI about your category, does it name you?

It works off your profile. Once you've told Recover who you are and who you sell to, it works out the questions your ideal customers are likely to ask an LLM. You can narrow those to specific niches or markets, or, if you're just starting out, ask for a set of foundational questions to get you going.

From that, it generates 50 to 60 questions. You can edit them, add your own, cut the ones that miss. Then it fires every question at each of the four leading models, ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity and Gemini, and waits for the answers. Fair warning: running that many questions across four models takes a while, so this isn't instant.

When it's done, the results are analysed into a plain picture: which questions name you, which name a competitor instead, and which return someone else entirely. That picture becomes a content gap analysis, the questions you should own but don't, which is the start of your content strategy. Build picks it up from there.
